Salary Slip Calculator FAQs

How is net salary calculated on a salary slip?

Net pay is total earnings payable for the period minus employee deductions such as PF, ESI, professional tax, TDS, advances and other deductions entered.

Should employer PF be deducted from employee take-home pay?

No. Employer PF is an employer-side contribution and is shown separately. Employee PF is deducted from the employee's salary where applicable.

Does this create an official or legally compliant payslip?

This tool creates a printable layout from your inputs. It does not verify payroll records, statutory compliance, attendance, tax calculations or employer-specific requirements. Employers should use their approved payroll process and required records.

Does this calculator calculate statutory PF, ESI or tax amounts automatically?

No. Enter amounts confirmed by payroll. PF wage base and membership, EPS eligibility, ESI coverage and contribution periods, state professional tax and TDS depend on employee-specific details and applicable rules. This tool does not determine statutory liability.

Is employee information uploaded or saved?

The calculator runs in the browser and does not send the entered payslip details to a server. The information is not saved by this page; print the result before closing.

How to read an Indian salary slip

A payslip records earnings payable, deductions withheld from the employee, and (where shown) employer-side contributions. Employer PF is part of employer cost, not an employee deduction.

SectionTypical itemsHow to treat it
EarningsBasic, DA, HRA, special allowance, overtime, bonus, arrearsAmounts payable for the salary period. Components may be fixed, variable or attendance-linked.
ReimbursementsTravel or business-expense claimsShow separately where payroll does so. A reimbursement is not automatically statutory wages.
Employee deductionsEmployee PF, employee ESI, professional tax, TDS, advancesSubtract these from employee earnings to arrive at net pay.
Employer contributionsEmployer EPF, EPS, employer ESI, NPS and benefitsShow separately. These do not reduce net pay; they may form part of employer cost / CTC.

Statutory payroll items: what to verify

  • PF: The mandatory-coverage wage ceiling was revised to ₹25,000 per month from 17 September 2026. Coverage, prior membership, PF wage base and EPS eligibility still matter; do not apply 12% to gross salary automatically. EPFO / PIB update.
  • EPS: EPS is an allocation from the employer-side contribution for eligible members, not an additional employee deduction. Scheme rules determine eligibility and pensionable wages.
  • ESI: The general monthly wage ceiling is ₹21,000 (with a higher ceiling for employees with disabilities); contribution-period continuation rules can apply when wages cross the ceiling mid-period. Enter amounts confirmed by payroll. ESIC FAQ.
  • Professional tax: State / UT law controls applicability, slabs, exemptions and payment frequency. Enter the amount applicable to the employee's work location and payroll month.
  • TDS: Enter actual payroll withholding. It depends on projected annual taxable income, tax regime, declarations, perquisites and year-to-date tax already deducted. This payslip tool is not an income-tax calculator.

The Labour Codes' 50% wage rule is not a gross-salary shortcut

From 21 November 2025, the Labour Codes' definition of wages includes basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance, if any. If specified excluded allowances exceed 50% of relevant remuneration, the excess is added back to wages, subject to the statutory definition and exclusions. This does not mean every allowance or reimbursement is automatically wages, nor does it mean PF is always 12% of gross. Payroll must identify the relevant wage components and apply the rules for the particular benefit. Ministry of Labour FAQs.

Example: net pay versus employer cost

If monthly earnings are ₹55,000, employee deductions are ₹4,000 and employer-side contributions / benefits are ₹3,000, net pay is ₹51,000 (₹55,000 − ₹4,000). Estimated employer cost is ₹58,000 (₹55,000 + ₹3,000), not ₹54,000. This simplified example assumes all amounts are for the same month and excludes other employer costs.
