What is EMI?

Equated Monthly Instalment (EMI) is the fixed monthly amount a borrower pays to repay a loan over an agreed tenure. For a standard fixed-rate reducing-balance loan, each instalment contains an interest component and a principal component. The interest portion generally falls as the outstanding principal reduces.

The standard EMI formula is:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

Here, P is the loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12 and 100), and n is the total number of monthly instalments. When the interest rate is zero, EMI is simply principal divided by tenure in months.

EMI calculator FAQs

How do I calculate my monthly EMI?

Enter the loan amount, annual interest rate and repayment tenure. The calculator converts the annual rate into a monthly rate and applies the reducing-balance EMI formula. It then shows the estimated monthly instalment, total interest and total repayment.

What is the EMI for a ₹30 lakh home loan?

It depends on the interest rate and tenure. For example, a ₹30 lakh loan at 8.5% per year for 20 years has an estimated EMI of about ₹26,035 per month using a monthly reducing-balance calculation. Change the rate and tenure above to estimate your own home loan EMI; the example is not a lender quote.

How much EMI will I pay for a ₹10 lakh car loan?

Your car loan EMI depends on the rate and repayment period. As an illustration, ₹10 lakh at 9.5% per year for 5 years works out to approximately ₹21,002 per month. Select Car and enter the actual rate and tenure offered by your lender for a personalised estimate.

How is personal loan EMI calculated?

Personal loan EMI is calculated from the principal, monthly interest rate and number of instalments, just like other standard reducing-balance loans. Since personal loan rates and fees vary by lender and borrower profile, use the rate in your loan offer rather than relying on a preset.

Does a longer loan tenure reduce EMI?

Generally, yes. Spreading the same principal over more months lowers the monthly EMI, but you usually pay more total interest because the balance remains outstanding for longer. Compare both the monthly instalment and total interest before choosing a tenure.

Why does the interest component make up more of my first EMIs?

Interest is calculated on the outstanding loan balance. At the start, the balance is highest, so a larger part of each EMI goes towards interest. As you repay principal and the balance falls, the interest portion generally decreases and the principal portion increases.

Does part-payment or prepayment reduce EMI or loan tenure?

A part-payment reduces the outstanding principal. Depending on your lender's rules and the option you select, the lender may reduce your remaining tenure while keeping the EMI similar, or reduce the EMI while keeping the tenure. This calculator's extra-monthly-payment estimate assumes the regular EMI stays unchanged and the loan closes earlier. Check applicable charges and terms with your lender.

Can I calculate EMI when the loan has a floating interest rate?

You can estimate repayments using the current rate, but this calculator does not predict future rate resets. If the benchmark or lender spread changes, your lender may revise the EMI, the remaining tenure or both. Recalculate with the revised rate for an updated estimate. For equated-instalment personal loans, RBI requires regulated lenders to communicate the impact and available options when rates reset.

Does the EMI amount include processing fees, insurance or GST?

No. The EMI result covers principal and interest only. The optional processing-fee field displays a separate upfront estimate; it is not added to the loan principal or EMI. Insurance, stamp duty, taxes, GST on applicable fees and other lender charges may add to the overall borrowing cost.

Why is my bank's EMI different from this EMI calculator?

The result is an estimate based on the figures you enter and a monthly reducing-balance method. A lender's amount may differ because of the final sanctioned rate, disbursement date, broken-period interest, payment dates, rounding, rate resets or loan-specific repayment rules. Use the lender's repayment schedule as the final figure.

What is the difference between flat interest and reducing-balance interest?

With a reducing-balance loan, interest is calculated on the outstanding principal, which falls as you repay. With a flat-rate quote, interest is calculated on the original principal for the agreed period. The quoted percentages are not directly comparable, so ask the lender for the total repayment or annual percentage rate before comparing offers. This calculator uses the reducing-balance method.

Can I use this calculator for an education loan during the moratorium period?

Use it to estimate a regular monthly repayment after repayment begins. It does not model course-period or moratorium interest, interest capitalisation, grace periods, step-up instalments or lender-specific repayment holidays. Check your education-loan sanction terms for the amount due when the moratorium ends.
