Current Income Tax Slabs in India — Tax Year 2026–27

Reviewed 23 November 2026 · New and old regimes, rebates, surcharge and historical FY 2025–26 rates

Compare your salary tax using the Income Tax Comparison Calculator. Estimate monthly take-home after PF and payroll deductions with the CTC to In-Hand Calculator. For salary components, read How CTC Is Calculated.

Tax Year 2026–27 is the first tax year under the Income-tax Act, 2025, which took effect on 1 April 2026. The new regime is the default regime. The old regime remains available to eligible taxpayers who follow the prescribed opt-out procedure.

New-regime quick view: Taxable income up to ₹4 lakh is in the nil slab. Eligible resident individuals may receive a section 156 rebate of up to ₹60,000 where total income does not exceed ₹12 lakh. For a resident salaried individual with only ordinary salary income, the ₹75,000 standard deduction can make gross salary up to ₹12.75 lakh tax-free.

Section 156 rebate: Eligible resident individuals can claim a rebate up to ₹60,000 when total income does not exceed ₹12 lakh, subject to statutory conditions. Marginal relief applies just above the threshold. The rebate is not a blanket exemption for every type of income; special-rate income can change the tax result.

Surcharge on income tax

Surcharge is charged on the income-tax amount, not directly on income. The maximum surcharge differs by regime. Special-rate income and specified categories can have separate caps and rules.

A 4% Health and Education Cess is applied to income tax plus surcharge. Marginal relief may apply at surcharge thresholds.

Historical rates: FY 2025–26 (AY 2026–27)

The following financial year has ended. Keep this historical reference separate from Tax Year 2026–27 when checking past salary or return records. The new-regime slab thresholds and rates for FY 2025–26 were the same: nil up to ₹4 lakh, then 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25% and 30% across successive ₹4 lakh bands. The former Income-tax Act, 1961 applied for that financial year, including the section 87A rebate rules then in force.

For historical FY 2025–26 salary calculations, remember that the new-regime standard deduction was ₹75,000 and the old-regime standard deduction was ₹50,000. Do not confuse FY 2025–26 / AY 2026–27 with the current Tax Year 2026–27 under the Income-tax Act, 2025.

Income tax slab FAQs

What are the current income tax slabs in India for Tax Year 2026–27?
Under the new regime, taxable income up to ₹4 lakh is nil, followed by 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25% and 30% across successive ₹4 lakh bands. The old regime uses age-based basic exemption limits and higher slab rates.
Is income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free under the new regime?
Eligible resident individuals may receive a section 156 rebate up to ₹60,000 where total income does not exceed ₹12 lakh. For a resident salaried individual with only ordinary salary income, the ₹75,000 standard deduction can make gross salary up to ₹12.75 lakh tax-free.
What is the old-regime basic exemption for senior citizens?
For resident individuals under the old regime, the basic exemption is generally ₹3 lakh for ages 60–79 and ₹5 lakh for age 80 or above. Non-residents do not receive these higher age-based thresholds.
What were the income tax slabs for FY 2025–26?
For historical FY 2025–26, the new-regime slab rates were nil up to ₹4 lakh, then 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25% and 30% across successive ₹4 lakh bands. That financial year used the Income-tax Act, 1961; Tax Year 2026–27 uses the Income-tax Act, 2025.
Is there a fixed deduction amount where the old regime is better?
No. The better regime depends on salary, age, residence, HRA, eligible deductions, other income and special-rate items. Use the calculator with your own details.
