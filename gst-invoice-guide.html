GST Tax Invoice in India: Rule 46, HSN/SAC, GST Rates and e-Invoice

Reviewed 23 November 2026 · Practical guide for Indian GST-registered businesses

Create a domestic invoice using the Free GST Invoice Generator. This guide explains the GST rules behind the fields you enter, including HSN/SAC, place of supply and e-invoice reporting.

A GST tax invoice is a prescribed document issued by a registered supplier for a taxable supply of goods or services. It records the supplier and recipient details, what was supplied, the taxable value and the GST charged. It also supports the recipient's input tax credit claim when the legal conditions are met.

Important: A printable invoice PDF is not automatically a GST e-invoice. If e-invoicing applies to the supplier and transaction, the covered document must be reported to an authorised Invoice Registration Portal (IRP) and receive an Invoice Reference Number (IRN) and signed QR code.

1. GST tax invoice vs bill of supply

A registered supplier making a taxable supply generally issues a tax invoice. A registered composition taxpayer or a registered supplier making an exempt supply generally issues a bill of supply instead. A person who is not registered under GST cannot issue a GST tax invoice or collect GST as tax.

DocumentTypical useGST charged on document?
Tax invoiceRegistered supplier making a taxable supplyYes, at the applicable rate
Bill of supplyComposition taxpayer or supplier of exempt supplies, where applicableNo GST amount is charged as tax
Commercial invoiceOrdinary billing document used by an unregistered personCannot show GST collected as tax

2. Mandatory particulars under Rule 46

Rule 46 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Rules, 2017 prescribes the particulars of a tax invoice. The precise requirements can vary with the transaction and applicable exceptions, but a supplier should review the following before issuing an invoice:

ParticularWhat to check
Supplier identityLegal name, registered address and GSTIN
Invoice number and dateConsecutive serial number, not exceeding 16 characters, unique for the financial year; date of issue
Recipient identityName, address and GSTIN/UIN where registered; other recipient details as required for the transaction
HSN/SAC and descriptionCorrect goods HSN or services SAC, with a clear description
Quantity and unitQuantity and applicable unit/UQC for goods
Value and discountTotal value and taxable value after eligible discounts or abatements
Tax particularsApplicable tax rate and amount of CGST, SGST/UTGST, IGST and cess, where applicable
Place of supplyPlace-of-supply state and state code for inter-state supplies
Reverse charge and authenticationWhether tax is payable on reverse charge basis and supplier signature/digital signature, subject to applicable exceptions

For an unregistered recipient, where the taxable value of the supply is ₹50,000 or more, Rule 46 also requires the recipient's name and address, delivery address, and the state name and code. For a lower-value supply, these particulars are required when the recipient asks for them. For the statutory text, read the CGST Rules, including Rule 46. CBIC also summarises the main tax-invoice particulars in its sectoral GST FAQs.

3. CGST, SGST/UTGST and IGST explained

GST follows a destination-based structure. For a domestic intra-state supply, the applicable GST is generally split into Central GST (CGST) and State GST (SGST) or Union Territory GST (UTGST). For an inter-state supply, Integrated GST (IGST) generally applies.

Example on taxable value ₹10,000 at 18%Tax calculationTotal invoice value
Intra-state₹900 CGST + ₹900 SGST₹11,800
Inter-state₹1,800 IGST₹11,800

The supplier's location and the legally determined place of supply decide whether a supply is intra-state or inter-state. Do not automatically use the buyer's billing address as the place of supply in every case. Services, immovable property, events, transportation and other special categories can have specific place-of-supply rules under the IGST Act.

4. HSN and SAC codes: goods vs services

HSN (Harmonised System of Nomenclature) classifies goods. SAC (Services Accounting Code) identifies services. Use the code that matches the actual supply, not merely a similar product or service description. A wrong code can lead to a wrong tax rate, return mismatch or input-tax-credit issue.

For reporting in GSTR-1 Table 12, GSTN's updated advisory generally requires a minimum of 4 HSN digits where the preceding financial year's aggregate annual turnover is up to ₹5 crore, and 6 digits where it is above ₹5 crore. This is a return-reporting validation rule; do not treat it as permission to use an incorrect or truncated classification on an invoice.

Search the code using the official GST portal HSN/SAC search and read the GSTN HSN validation advisory.

5. GST rates in India: why you must check the HSN/SAC

GST rates are classification-specific and are notified by the Government. The rate rationalisation that took effect from 22 September 2025 established 5% and 18% as the two main rates, alongside a special 40% rate for selected goods and services. Exemptions and other notified treatments can still apply, and later notifications may amend individual entries.

Therefore, do not assume that every product is taxed at 5% or 18%, or select a rate based only on a broad product category. Check the current CBIC GST goods and services rate schedule and relevant notifications for the exact HSN/SAC and conditions. The Government's October 2026 overview of next-generation GST reforms provides the broad current structure.

6. Who needs to generate an e-invoice?

E-invoicing is the reporting of specified GST documents to a notified IRP to obtain an IRN and signed QR code. It does not mean that the Government portal creates the invoice for the business; the supplier continues to prepare the invoice in its own billing or accounting system and reports the required data.

The general turnover threshold is aggregate turnover exceeding ₹5 crore in any preceding financial year from 2017–18 onward, calculated on a PAN basis, subject to notified exclusions and the documents/supplies covered by the rules. The GSTN overview explains the process and exclusions. Check current notifications and your enablement status on the official GST e-Invoice portal.

MailFix generator limitation: This tool creates a printable invoice format only. It does not connect to an IRP, generate an IRN, create a signed QR code, file GSTR-1 or produce an e-way bill. If e-invoicing applies to you, complete the IRP reporting process using an authorised portal or compliant software.

7. Checklist before issuing a GST invoice

  • Confirm the supplier is registered and the GSTIN is correct.
  • Use a consecutive invoice number of up to 16 characters that is unique for the financial year.
  • Confirm the recipient details and GSTIN, where applicable.
  • Verify the HSN/SAC and the current rate notification for each line item.
  • Determine the place of supply under the IGST Act before choosing CGST/SGST or IGST.
  • Check taxable value, eligible discounts, tax amount, reverse-charge status and any applicable cess.
  • Confirm whether e-invoice, e-way bill or another document requirement applies to the transaction.
  • Retain the issued invoice and supporting records in accordance with the applicable law.

8. Common GST invoice mistakes

  • Using the buyer's state without checking place-of-supply rules: this can lead to an incorrect intra-state/inter-state tax split.
  • Copying a rate from an old invoice: rate notifications change, and the same broad product category can contain different classifications or conditions.
  • Entering a truncated HSN: use the exact valid code shown in the GST portal master.
  • Assuming a PDF is an e-invoice: a covered e-invoice requires IRP reporting and a valid IRN/QR code.
  • Issuing a tax invoice for an exempt or composition supply: check whether a bill of supply is the required document instead.

GST tax invoice FAQs

Can an unregistered person issue a GST tax invoice?
No. A person who is not registered under GST cannot issue a GST tax invoice or collect GST as tax. They may issue an ordinary commercial invoice for the transaction.
What is the difference between a tax invoice and a bill of supply?
A tax invoice is generally issued by a registered supplier for a taxable supply. A registered composition taxpayer or supplier of exempt supplies generally issues a bill of supply instead; it does not show GST charged as tax.
What is the maximum GST invoice number length?
Rule 46 provides for a consecutive serial number not exceeding 16 characters, in one or multiple series, unique for the financial year. The permitted characters include alphabets, numerals, hyphen and slash.
Does this GST invoice generator create an IRN?
No. It creates a printable invoice layout only. Eligible taxpayers must report covered documents to an authorised Invoice Registration Portal to obtain an IRN and signed QR code.
Can I use the buyer's state as the place of supply?
Not in every transaction. Place of supply is determined under the IGST Act and may depend on the nature of the supply, recipient status, property location, event location or other special rules. Confirm the correct place of supply before generating the invoice.
