New vs Old Tax Regime in India — Which Is Better?

Reviewed 23 November 2026 · Current rates, deductions and salary examples

Compare both regimes with the Income Tax Comparison Calculator. For estimated monthly take-home after PF and payroll deductions, use the CTC to In-Hand Calculator. Read the current and historical income tax slab guide.

The new tax regime is the default regime for individuals under the Income-tax Act, 2025. It offers lower slab rates and a higher standard deduction for salary, but most common exemptions and deductions available under the old regime are generally not available. The right choice depends on your actual income, eligible deductions, age, residence and other taxable income.

New-regime rebate: Eligible resident individuals may receive a section 156 rebate of up to ₹60,000 when total income does not exceed ₹12 lakh, subject to statutory conditions. For a resident salaried individual with only ordinary salary income, the ₹75,000 standard deduction can make gross salary up to ₹12.75 lakh tax-free. Special-rate income and other income can change the outcome.

Can salaried employees switch regimes?

Employees without business or professional income can generally choose the regime each year when filing their return, subject to the applicable procedure and due date. Individuals with business or professional income face additional restrictions on opting out of the default regime and changing that choice. Check the current Income Tax Department instructions before filing.

For the legal framework and current rates, read the official Tax Year 2026–27 tax-rate memorandum and the Income Tax Slabs guide. For salary structure, read How CTC Is Calculated.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which tax regime is the default in Tax Year 2026–27?
The new tax regime is the default regime under the Income-tax Act, 2025. An eligible taxpayer may opt out and use the old regime by following the prescribed procedure.
Is gross salary up to ₹12.75 lakh tax-free?
For an eligible resident salaried individual with only ordinary salary income, the ₹75,000 standard deduction can reduce gross salary of ₹12.75 lakh to total income of ₹12 lakh, where the section 156 rebate may eliminate tax. Other income and special-rate items can change this result.
Does age affect the new regime?
No. New-regime slab thresholds are age-neutral. Age-based higher basic exemption limits apply under the old regime to resident senior and super senior citizens.
Is there a fixed deduction amount where the old regime becomes better?
No. The result depends on the taxpayer's income, age, residence, eligible exemptions and deductions, other income and special-rate items. Compare the actual tax estimates rather than relying on a fixed threshold.
Which deductions are generally unavailable under the new regime?
Common claims such as 80C investments, HRA exemption, 80D health-insurance deduction and self-occupied home-loan interest are generally not available under the new regime. Some specific deductions, including eligible employer NPS contributions, remain available under statutory limits.
