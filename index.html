Why Hinglish Email Writing is Harder Than It Looks

Most Indian professionals think in Hindi (or their regional language) and write in English. The result is a constant mental translation that slows down writing, introduces awkward phrasing, and often comes across as either too casual or too stiff — depending on which words slip through.

A message like "Sir please do the needful and revert at earliest" is perfectly clear to another Indian reader but sounds strange to a global audience. Conversely, trying to write formal British English from scratch feels unnatural and often produces the wrong tone — too cold for Indian workplace culture, where a touch of warmth is expected even in professional communication.

Hinglish is not broken English — it is a dialect. It has its own grammar patterns, idioms, and social registers. MailFix's email fixer understands this. When you write "bhai kal tak ka kaam nahi hua please extension chahiye", it doesn't just translate — it reads the intent (requesting a deadline extension), infers the relationship (colleague, not manager), and writes an appropriate email with the right level of formality, a clear subject line, and a professional closing.

This is why MailFix works where generic AI tools like ChatGPT fall short for Indian users. You don't have to prompt-engineer or explain context. The tool knows what you mean.
