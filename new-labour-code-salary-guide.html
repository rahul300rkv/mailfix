New Labour Codes in India: What Changes for Your Salary?

Understand the four Labour Codes, the revised definition of wages, the 50% allowance rule and the practical checks to make on your salary structure and payslip.

Legal position reviewed 23 November 2026 · Ministry guidance and notifications cited below

First, what is the current position?

India's four Labour Codes consolidate 29 central labour laws. The Government brought all four Codes into effect from 21 November 2025. The Ministry has since published Central Rules, notifications and FAQs; the current position must be read with the relevant State rules and establishment-specific coverage.

That does not mean every employee's payslip must look identical. The applicable government (Central or State), the establishment's coverage, the relevant Code and Rules, and any more favourable employment terms can all matter. Existing rules continue during transition to the extent they are consistent with the Codes.

Important distinction: The 50% rule is part of the statutory definition of wages. It is not a universal instruction that every employer must make basic salary exactly 50% of CTC.

What exactly is the 50% wage rule?

The statutory definition starts with remuneration payable to an employee and specifically includes basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance, if any. It also identifies certain exclusions. If the specified excluded allowances and benefits exceed 50% of total remuneration (or another notified percentage), the excess is added back to wages for statutory calculations.

In practical terms, a large allowance-heavy structure may produce a statutory wage figure higher than the basic-plus-DA figure shown in the salary breakup. The test is not simply “basic must be half of CTC”. The Ministry's clarifications explain that statutory employer PF/pension contributions and statutory bonus are included when arriving at the remuneration base, while gratuity, ESI and other retirement benefits are not. Overtime allowance also forms part of the calculation. The detailed classification of a component depends on the statutory wording, not simply its payroll label.

Some important inclusions and exclusions

  • Basic pay, DA and retaining allowance are included in wages.
  • Annual performance-based incentives, ESOPs, variable components and reimbursement-based payments are identified by the Ministry as excluded from wages.
  • Overtime allowance payment forms part of the 50% calculation; if the relevant allowance components cross the threshold, the excess is added back.
  • Leave encashment is not part of allowances for this test; a one-time leave encashment payment is not included in wages.
  • Food coupons and meal vouchers can be treated as remuneration in kind. The Ministry says in-kind benefits up to 15% of remuneration are included in wages.
  • Employer-paid insurance premiums, employer-provided accommodation and certain other facilities are treated differently from cash allowances; classification depends on the statutory clauses.
Do not classify a component from its payslip label alone. A payment's actual purpose, whether it is recurring or reimbursed against bills, and the relevant statutory exclusion can change its treatment.

How can this affect PF, gratuity and take-home pay?

Provident fund (PF)

The revised wage definition is relevant to social-security calculations, but PF coverage and contributions are still governed by the Social Security Code, the EPF schemes and notifications. The wage ceiling for mandatory coverage under Chapter III of the Code on Social Security was raised from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 per month with effect from 17 September 2026. This is a coverage threshold, not a statement that every existing EPF member's contribution must stop at ₹25,000. Membership status, prior coverage, higher-wage arrangements and the applicable scheme provisions can change the result.

The general EPF contribution rate is commonly 12% of the applicable contribution wages for the employee and employer, subject to statutory exceptions and scheme rules. Do not calculate the contribution simply as 12% of CTC or assume that the entire employer contribution is credited to the employee's EPF account; the employer share is allocated under the applicable EPF/EPS provisions.

The Ministry's October 2026 FAQs state that the employer EPF contribution is 12% of wages payable and the employee contribution is equal to the employer contribution. The same FAQ compilation says establishments with 20 or more employees are covered for Chapter III purposes. Voluntary contributions above the notified wage ceiling are permitted under the applicable scheme provisions.

Gratuity

The revised wage definition applies to gratuity from 21 November 2025. For an eligible employee covered by the standard monthly-rated formula, gratuity is generally calculated as last-drawn monthly wages × 15 ÷ 26 × eligible years of service. A completed year and a part of a year exceeding six months are counted as prescribed by the Code. The current notified maximum is ₹20 lakh, unless a more favourable entitlement applies.

Example: If the relevant last-drawn monthly wages are ₹30,000 and eligible service is 6 years, the indicative amount is ₹30,000 × 15 ÷ 26 × 6 = ₹1,03,846 (rounded). This is an illustration, not an eligibility decision; the statutory wage definition, service record, employee category and special cases must be checked.

Fixed-term employment: A directly engaged fixed-term employee becomes eligible after rendering one year of service under the contract, and gratuity is paid pro rata. The Ministry clarifies that fixed-term employment in this context means direct employment by the employer; contract labour engaged through a contractor is treated separately, with the contractor responsible for gratuity on completion of five years of continuous service under the applicable provision.

Leave, carry-forward and encashment

Do not assume that the OSH & Working Conditions Code makes the same statutory leave-encashment rule available to every person on a company payroll. Ministry FAQs state that the relevant leave provisions apply to workers and to supervisors drawing wages not exceeding ₹18,000 per month (or a subsequently notified amount). The statutory definition of worker includes specified sales-promotion employees and working journalists. A person in a managerial role, or a supervisor above the threshold, should check the applicable State Shops and Establishments law, service rules, award, agreement and employment contract.

The Ministry FAQs state that a worker may carry forward up to 30 days of earned leave to the succeeding calendar year; leave applied for but refused by the employer may be carried forward without that limit. Separate rules govern when leave wages are payable and when leave is encashed. The 50% wage definition FAQ also clarifies that leave encashment is not an allowance for the wage add-back test.

Bonus and other statutory benefits

The definition of wages is not the same as the eligibility test for every benefit. Bonus eligibility, wage ceilings, PF/ESI coverage and other conditions are separately governed by the relevant Code, notifications and rules. A higher wage base does not automatically mean every employee receives every benefit.

Will monthly take-home salary fall?

Not automatically. If the statutory wage base increases, an employee's PF contribution may increase where the applicable contribution rules require it; employer contributions and gratuity liability may also change. The final effect depends on salary design, contribution ceilings, coverage and whether the employer changes the CTC or fixed-gross structure. Tax, professional tax and other deductions are separate calculations.

Use the calculators as estimates, not legal determinations. Compare your offer's components with the Salary Breakup Calculator, then estimate tax and deductions with the CTC to In-Hand Calculator. Confirm statutory bases with HR/payroll.

Resignation: when must unpaid wages be paid?

Section 17(2) of the Code on Wages, 2019 requires wages payable to an employee who is removed, dismissed, retrenched or resigns to be paid within two working days of the relevant event. The Ministry's October 2026 FAQ compilation expressly clarifies that Section 17(2) provides for payment of wages within two days and does not specify payment of other components within that timeline. Therefore, do not describe the rule as a blanket two-day deadline for every full-and-final settlement item, such as gratuity, leave encashment, reimbursements, bonus or disputed recoveries. Those amounts may be governed by separate provisions, rules, contracts and the facts of the exit.

For monthly wage periods, the ordinary payment deadline is before expiry of the seventh day of the succeeding month. The Code also prescribes different schedules for daily, weekly and fortnightly wage periods. Keep the resignation acknowledgement, last working day confirmation, payslips and bank credit records.

What should an employee check in the next payslip?

  1. Identify the wage components: note basic pay, DA, retaining allowance and each recurring allowance separately.
  2. Ask how the 50% test is calculated: request the monthly remuneration base and the add-back, if any, rather than assuming basic must equal 50% of CTC.
  3. Check PF details: confirm membership, contribution wage, applicable ceiling and employee/employer contributions on the payslip and EPFO passbook.
  4. Check gratuity treatment: understand the wage base used for the provision and the applicable service/eligibility rules.
  5. Separate CTC from cash salary: employer PF, gratuity provision, insurance and variable pay may be part of CTC but are not necessarily monthly cash paid to you.
  6. Ask which rules apply: confirm whether your establishment falls under the Central or State sphere and whether any more favourable award, agreement or contract applies.

Keep your offer letter, salary annexure, payslips, PF passbook and written HR clarifications. If a material discrepancy remains, seek advice from a qualified labour-law professional or the appropriate labour authority.

Common myths — corrected

“Basic salary must be 50% of CTC.”

Incorrect. The rule is an add-back test on specified allowances and benefits against total remuneration. CTC is not the statutory denominator by default.

“Everyone's take-home pay will reduce.”

Incorrect. Outcomes depend on the employee's structure, statutory coverage and contribution rules. There is no universal take-home reduction percentage.

“All allowances are excluded until the 50% limit.”

Too broad. The Code contains specific inclusions and exclusions, and the Ministry has issued clarifications on reimbursements, incentives, in-kind benefits and other components.

“The four Codes mean the same rule applies identically to every workplace.”

Incorrect. Appropriate Government, establishment coverage, Central or State Rules and the employee's category can affect the detailed position.

Frequently asked questions

Do the Labour Codes require basic salary to be exactly 50% of CTC?

No. The 50% provision is an add-back test for specified allowances and benefits against statutory remuneration. It does not prescribe a universal basic-salary percentage of CTC.

What is the EPF wage ceiling in 2026?

The wage ceiling for mandatory coverage under Chapter III of the Code on Social Security is ₹25,000 per month from 17 September 2026. Existing membership and higher-wage contribution arrangements require separate consideration under EPF scheme rules.

How is gratuity calculated under the Labour Codes?

For the standard monthly-rated formula, the indicative calculation is last-drawn statutory wages multiplied by 15/26 and by eligible years of service. Special rules apply to fixed-term, seasonal, piece-rated, deceased and disabled employees; the current notified ceiling is ₹20 lakh.

Does a fixed-term employee get gratuity after one year?

A directly engaged fixed-term employee is eligible after rendering one year of service under the contract, with gratuity calculated pro rata. The Ministry distinguishes this from contract labour engaged through a contractor.

Does every private-sector employee get statutory leave encashment under the OSH Code?

No. The Ministry's FAQs describe the relevant OSH Code leave provisions as applying to workers and supervisors within the stated wage threshold. State law, service rules and more favourable contractual terms may provide additional rights.

When should wages be paid after resignation?

Section 17(2) of the Code on Wages generally requires wages payable on resignation to be paid within two working days. This should not be confused with a guarantee that every full-and-final settlement component is resolved in two days.

Will everyone's take-home salary decrease?

No. The impact depends on salary components, statutory coverage, contribution bases, employer policy and applicable rules. A higher statutory wage base does not automatically reduce every employee's take-home pay.

Are annual performance incentives included in statutory wages?

The Ministry's FAQs state that annual performance-based incentives do not form part of wages. Other payment types must be classified under the statutory definition and relevant facts.

Does CTC mean the same thing as statutory wages?

No. CTC is an employer compensation and costing term; wages is a defined statutory term used for specific labour-law calculations.

When did the four Labour Codes take effect?

The Government brought the four Labour Codes into effect from 21 November 2025. Relevant Central or State rules, notifications and establishment coverage still need to be checked.
