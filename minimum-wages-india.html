Minimum Wages in India

Understand which government fixes the rate for a workplace, how to read the relevant notification, and what to verify before making a payroll or compliance decision.

There is no single minimum-wage figure that applies to every job in India. The applicable rate depends on the appropriate government, the establishment and employment covered by the notification, the worker category, location and effective period. This page is deliberately an educational guide—not a live rate finder, calculator or exhaustive State/UT directory.

Start here: identify the applicable government

First determine whether the establishment falls within the Central Government sphere or the relevant State/UT sphere. Do not decide this only from the employer’s brand, registered office or the fact that it operates in several states. The nature of the establishment, activity and statutory coverage matter.

  • Central sphere: certain specified sectors and establishments for which the Central Government is the appropriate government under the applicable law. Check the statutory definition and the establishment’s actual activity.
  • State/UT sphere: establishments for which the relevant State or Union Territory Government is the appropriate government. Start with the Labour Department or Labour Commissionerate for the state/UT where the employee works.
  • Not sure: ask the employer’s HR/payroll team which notification they apply and request the notification number and wage schedule. If the answer is unclear or disputed, seek guidance from the relevant labour department or a qualified labour-law professional.

The Ministry has stated in a Lok Sabha answer that state-wise minimum-wage data is not maintained centrally. That is one reason this guide does not attempt to reproduce a nationwide rate table. Read the Ministry’s Lok Sabha answer dated 3 August 2026.

How to find and read the correct notification

  1. Confirm the appropriate government. Establish whether the Central or State/UT government is responsible for the establishment and employment.
  2. Find the official notification. Use the relevant Labour Department, Labour Commissionerate, Chief Labour Commissioner (Central), Labour Bureau or State Gazette website. Prefer the original government PDF over news articles, blogs or salary websites.
  3. Match the scheduled employment or work category. Check that the notification covers the industry, occupation or establishment concerned. A similar-sounding category is not automatically equivalent.
  4. Match the worker’s skill classification. Notifications may distinguish unskilled, semi-skilled, skilled, highly skilled or other categories. Use the definitions in that notification.
  5. Match the geographical class. Check the exact zone, area, district or city classification. Do not assume a nearby city or district has the same rate.
  6. Read the wage components. Identify whether the amount is basic wage alone, basic plus Variable Dearness Allowance (VDA), or an all-inclusive rate. Follow the notification’s own calculation and inclusion rules.
  7. Check dates and later changes. Record the issue date, effective-from date, end date if any, later revisions, corrigenda and superseding orders. An old PDF may still appear in search results after it has been replaced.
  8. Use the correct wage period. Confirm whether the rate is daily, monthly or another unit and use the conversion method prescribed by the relevant notification or rules. Do not assume a universal number of working days.

What do basic wage and VDA mean?

Some notifications state a single consolidated minimum rate. Others show a basic rate and a separate Variable Dearness Allowance (VDA), which may be revised periodically using a specified consumer-price-index method. The notification determines which components apply and how they are added.

Do not add VDA twice, omit it where it is part of the notified rate, or substitute a general inflation figure. Check the relevant order’s table, formula, reference index and effective period.

Legal framework update · 2026

The Ministry of Labour & Employment lists the Code on Wages, 2019 and the Code on Wages (Central) Rules, 2026 on its Labour Codes page. The Central Rules were notified by G.S.R. 343(E) dated 8 May 2026. Do not assume that a Central Rule automatically supplies the rate table for every State or scheduled employment: use the appropriate government's applicable rate notification and check State rules and transition provisions.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using a rate from a different State, zone, scheduled employment or skill category.
  • Assuming a Central Government rate applies just because the employer is a large or national company.
  • Using a third-party summary without checking the original government notification.
  • Applying a VDA figure from the wrong revision period or adding it twice.
  • Using an expired or superseded notification because it is still indexed by a search engine.
  • Converting daily and monthly rates using an assumed divisor instead of the prescribed method.
  • Comparing minimum wages directly with CTC or take-home pay without considering wage definitions and payroll components.

What to record when checking a notification

  • Appropriate government and establishment coverage
  • State/UT, district, zone or geographical class
  • Employment / occupation and skill category
  • Basic wage, VDA or other included components
  • Daily, hourly or monthly rate and prescribed conversion method
  • Notification number, publication date and effective-from date
  • End date, superseding order, corrigendum and arrears implications
  • Official source URL and the date you accessed it

For payroll decisions, retain a copy of the exact notification used. If an order is published after its stated effective date, check whether arrears are due for the intervening period.

Important distinction: minimum wages are not take-home salary

Minimum wages are statutory wage floors. They are not the same as CTC, gross salary or net take-home pay. Payroll deductions, employer contributions, overtime, leave, attendance and the statutory definition of wages can affect the final payslip. Use the applicable wage notification first, then calculate payroll using the correct legal and employment assumptions.

Minimum Wages in India FAQs

Is there one minimum wage for all of India?

No. The appropriate Central or State Government fixes rates within its jurisdiction. Rates can differ by area, skill category, type of work and effective date.

How do I know whether Central or State minimum wages apply?

Identify the establishment and the appropriate government under the applicable labour law. Central-sphere coverage is limited to qualifying establishments and sectors; most other establishments fall under the relevant State Government. Confirm the classification with the labour department or a qualified professional.

What is VDA in minimum wages?

Variable Dearness Allowance is a cost-of-living component linked to a consumer price index where the relevant government notification provides for it. Revision dates and calculations differ by jurisdiction.

Can I use an old minimum-wage notification?

Only if it remains applicable for the relevant period and has not been superseded. Check the notification number, issue date, effective date, validity period, corrigenda and later revisions.

Does this page provide legal advice or a guaranteed wage rate?

No. This is an educational guide, not a live rate database, calculator or legal opinion. Verify the latest official notification for the relevant jurisdiction, employment, skill category, area and wage period before making a payroll or compliance decision.
