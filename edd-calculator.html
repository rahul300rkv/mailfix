Understanding your pregnancy due date

Pregnancy gestational age is conventionally counted from the first day of the last menstrual period. The standard LMP method estimates 40 weeks (280 days) to the due date and assumes a 28-day cycle with ovulation around day 14.

Why ultrasound dating may differ

Cycle variation, uncertain LMP dates and timing of ovulation can affect an LMP estimate. An early ultrasound and your clinician's assessment may provide a more suitable estimated due date. Do not use this calculator to make medical decisions.

Frequently asked questions

How is the estimated due date calculated?

A common estimate adds 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of the last menstrual period. This assumes a 28-day cycle; the calculator adjusts for a different cycle length.

What if I do not know my last menstrual period date?

A clinician may estimate pregnancy dating using an early ultrasound, known conception or embryo-transfer dates and your medical history. This calculator cannot replace that assessment.

How many weeks pregnant am I?

Gestational age is commonly counted from the first day of the last menstrual period, not the date of conception. Enter your LMP date to estimate completed weeks and remaining days.

Is the due date exact?

No. It is an estimate. Many babies are born before or after the estimated date, and clinical dating may be revised after an ultrasound.

Does a longer or shorter menstrual cycle change the due date?

It can change the LMP-based estimate. This calculator adjusts the standard 280-day estimate by the difference between your cycle length and 28 days.

When should I contact a healthcare professional?

Arrange antenatal care to confirm pregnancy dating and follow your clinician's advice. Seek urgent medical care for concerning symptoms such as heavy bleeding, severe abdominal pain, fainting or other emergency symptoms.
